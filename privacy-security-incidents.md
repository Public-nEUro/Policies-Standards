# Privacy, security and incidents

Principles for protection shall, according to GDPR [Article 5(1)](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-5-gdpr/), include purpose limitation, data minimisation, storage minimisation, and integrity and confidentiality. This means that personal data should be processed only for specified legal purposes, limited to what is relevant and necessary, not kept longer than necessary, and protected through appropriate technical and organisational measures.

GDPR [Article 32(1)](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-32-gdpr/) requires data controllers to implement appropriate technical and organisational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk. This assessment must take into account the state of the art, the costs of implementation, the nature, scope, context and purposes of processing, and the likelihood and severity of risks to the rights and freedoms of natural persons.

GDPR [Article 32(1)](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-32-gdpr/) suggests encryption and/or pseudonymisation when suitable. Pseudonymisation, according to GDPR [Article 4(5)](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-4-gdpr/), means processing personal data so that they can no longer be attributed to a specific person without additional information, such as a key, which must be kept protected and separate. If this additional information is deleted so that attribution is no longer possible even indirectly, the data may become anonymous and cease to be personal data regulated by GDPR. GDPR [Article 32(1)(d)](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-32-gdpr/) also requires regular assessment of the effectiveness of protective measures, since changes in the state of the art and availability of additional information may make previously anonymous data attributable again.

## Privacy

PublicnEUro supports FAIR and GDPR-compliant sharing of brain-imaging research data. The institution responsible for a dataset remains the data controller for that dataset and is responsible for ensuring that the data were collected lawfully, that the data have been properly pseudonymised before upload, and that the dataset is accompanied by a nationally lawful and GDPR-compliant Data User Agreement (DUA).

NRU, operating PublicnEUro, normally processes dataset data on behalf of the data controller under the service agreement and Data Processing Agreement. NRU processes personal data only according to documented instructions from the data controller and only to the extent necessary to provide the agreed services.

PublicnEUro also processes repository-user personal information needed to operate the service. This includes information provided when a person registers as a user, sets up a service contract as a dataset author, or is listed by a dataset author as part of a private hosting service. The user privacy notice describes these data and the rights of users in more detail: [PublicnEUro Privacy Notice](user-privacy-notice.md).

Access to controlled datasets is granted on a dataset-by-dataset basis. Users and their institutions must be identifiable unless the dataset is fully open. Before downloading controlled data, users must sign the dataset-specific DUA and, where required for non-EU/EEA access from countries without an adequate level of protection, the relevant EU Standard Contractual Clauses.

PublicnEUro may send user information related to dataset access to data authors/data controllers and to supervisory authorities for audit purposes. Signed DUAs, Standard Contractual Clauses and user access records are retained so that access decisions remain auditable.

## Security

PublicnEUro applies technical and organisational measures intended to protect the confidentiality, integrity, availability and resilience of the repository service and the data it processes. The Data Processing Agreement requires measures consistent with GDPR Article 32 and uses ISO 27001 principles and recommendations as a framework guideline.

Datasets are deposited through secured transfer routes and are checked for compliance before release. Dataset files are hosted using Computerome, NRU's approved sub-processor for data hosting and computation resources. Computerome is located in Denmark, provides secured read/write access, encryption in transit and geo-redundant backups, and is described in the service agreement as meeting compliance requirements for EU health-data storage.

PublicnEUro limits access to identified and authorised personnel. Personnel access is individual and logged so that operations performed on data are uniquely identifiable. Only persons who need access for the relevant processing purpose, auditing, operational or systems tasks should be authorised. Access and authorisations must be reviewed, and authorisation must be cancelled when it is no longer required.

Registered user access is controlled through account registration, manual validation and password-based login. New access also requires a One-Time Password generated by a third party. User status may be revoked after one year without activity and can be reactivated after checks such as confirming that the user remains at the same institution.

PublicnEUro maintains access and processing logs. The Data Processing Agreement requires logs to record, at minimum, the time, user and type of use. Logs are kept for at least six months and may be kept longer when needed for investigation or audit.

Security controls also include risk evaluation, measures to limit unauthorised access, procedures for restoring availability after a physical or technical incident, regular assessment of technical and organisational measures, confidentiality obligations for authorised personnel and sub-processors, and procedures for secure deletion or disposal when processing ceases.

## Incidents

PublicnEUro must have formal procedures for handling security incidents. Under the Data Processing Agreement, NRU must inform the data controller in writing and without unnecessary delay about deviations from the agreement, including deviations from documented instructions, deviations from agreed access to personal data, relevant deviations from planned releases/upgrades/tests, or reasonable suspicion of confidentiality breach, misuse, loss or deterioration of personal data.

When NRU becomes aware of a personal data breach connected to the Data Processing Agreement, whether at NRU or at a sub-processor, it must inform the data controller without unnecessary delay.

A personal data breach notification must include:

- the nature of the personal data breach and, if possible, who is affected;
- the number of affected data subjects and affected personal-data records, where known;
- the likely consequences of the breach;
- the measures NRU has taken or proposes to manage the breach;
- actions that can be taken to limit possible adverse effects.

NRU must assist the data controller with obligations for which the data controller is responsible, including responding to data-subject rights requests, fulfilling relevant legal obligations and carrying out a data protection impact assessment when assistance is necessary.

Where an interruption in operations occurs, the Data Processing Agreement states that the institution will be informed if the interruption lasts more than one week. Where a breach of the Data Processing Agreement occurs, the agreement forms part of the principal service agreement and material breach may allow cancellation under the contractual terms.
